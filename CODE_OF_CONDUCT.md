# Code of Conduct

*[Leer en español](CODIGO_DE_CONDUCTA.md)*

## Our commitment

ZAYNOR is built around a simple rule: consequential claims should be
supported by evidence, and uncertainty should remain uncertainty.

The same rule applies to collaboration.

Technical disagreement, adversarial review, failed hypotheses, negative
results, and corrections are normal here. Harassment, intimidation,
fabricated evidence, concealed conflicts, and attacks on contributors are not.

We are committed to a harassment-free environment for everyone, regardless
of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression,
level of experience, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion,
sexual identity or orientation, or other protected characteristic.

## Expected behavior

### Challenge claims, not people

Be direct. Strong technical disagreement is welcome.

If you believe a design, security claim, forensic conclusion, test, or patch
is wrong, identify what fails and provide the strongest evidence you have:
code references, reproduction steps, test output, counterexamples, traces,
artifacts, or a falsifiable argument.

"This is wrong" may begin a review. It should not end one.

### Separate evidence from interpretation

Distinguish clearly between:

- what the code does;
- what a test demonstrated;
- what an artifact contains;
- what you infer from those facts;
- what remains unknown.

Do not present assumptions, generated content, model output, or unverified
claims as established fact.

### Make corrections auditable

Mistakes are expected. Concealing them is not.

Correct inaccurate claims when they are discovered. Preserve enough context
for another contributor to understand what changed and why. A retracted or
narrowed claim supported by evidence is preferable to an impressive claim
that cannot survive verification.

### Preserve provenance and authorship

Do not alter, omit, fabricate, or misrepresent provenance, test results,
security findings, evidence, contribution history, or authorship.

Credit human contributors for their work. AI systems and automated tools may
assist development and analysis, but their output must not be presented as
independent corroboration or used to erase the provenance of human work.

### Respect security and forensic boundaries

Treat evidence, logs, tool output, issue content, model output, and code under
review as untrusted data where appropriate.

Do not use project collaboration as authorization to:

- access systems, accounts, evidence, or data you were not authorized to access;
- publish private case material, credentials, exploits, or personal information;
- expand a proof of concept beyond the agreed test environment;
- bypass responsible-disclosure procedures;
- manipulate project evidence or audit records.

Security vulnerabilities belong under [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md), not in
public issues containing exploit details.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not acceptable in project spaces or while representing
the project:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, insults, or personal attacks;
- discrimination or derogatory conduct;
- sexualized language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention;
- publishing another person's private information without explicit permission;
- knowingly fabricating or falsifying evidence, test output, attribution, or
  security findings;
- deliberately misrepresenting another contributor's work or authorship;
- retaliation against someone for reporting a vulnerability, correcting a
  claim, disagreeing technically, or reporting conduct under this policy;
- conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional
  research, engineering, or forensic environment.

Technical criticism — including adversarial review of code written by a
maintainer — is not harassment when it addresses the work rather than the
person.

## Scope

This Code applies to project-controlled spaces, including issues, pull
requests, reviews, discussions, commits, documentation, and project-managed
communication channels.

It also applies when someone is explicitly representing ZAYNOR in a public
space.

It does not turn ordinary private disagreements between contributors into
project governance matters merely because the people involved also contribute
to ZAYNOR.

## Enforcement

Conduct reports should be sent privately to the maintainer. Do not publish
sensitive details in a public issue.

Reports should include enough information to understand the conduct, where it
occurred, and any relevant context or records. Reports will be reviewed as
privately as practical, with attention to both the safety of participants and
the available evidence.

Responses may include a warning, removal of content, restriction from project
spaces, temporary suspension, or permanent exclusion, depending on the
conduct and circumstances.

No contributor, including a maintainer, is exempt from this Code.

Security vulnerabilities should instead follow the private reporting process
in [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md).
